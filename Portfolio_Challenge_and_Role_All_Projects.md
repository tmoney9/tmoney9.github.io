## 1. Design Tokens for Amazon

### Challenge
At Amazon, I was working on an internal design system used by over 300 teams with 150+ designers building employee-facing experiences like HR services, pay and benefits, the learning portal, and more.

In 2024, the design system was preparing for its largest platform release, with a hard deadline for design work to be finished by mid-September. Design tokens were a foundational dependency for this release and future initiatives, including theming, dark, dense, and other modes, and more.

However, the existing token architecture, implemented earlier in the year, contained critical structural issues that put the release and long-term system health at risk.

### My Role
I stepped in to lead the diagnosis, correction, and stabilization of the token system under significant time pressure. I owned end-to-end responsibility for:

- Auditing the existing token architecture.
- Identifying systemic failures and scalability risks.
- Redesigning the token model and naming strategy.
- Leading alignment across design and engineering.
- Establishing governance & documentation.
- Driving implementation readiness.

## 2. SaaS Design Framework

### Challenge
The Detail View, showing data on deals, contacts, or organizations, is Pipedrive’s most visited screen, with over 105,000 users and 1.8 million weekly visits only to the deal details. As the company expanded its product portfolio, this screen had become a bottleneck: inconsistent UX across teams, growing technical debt, and no scalable way to support future product lines.

My goal was to move from a static, hard-coded page to a scalable framework library that could support any object type across current and future product lines.

### My Role
I inherited the initiative at a fragile moment. The original designer had left the company, taking much of the design rationale with them. There was no documentation, no shared vision, and no alignment between design, product, and engineering. 

I stepped in as the lead designer, responsible for stabilizing the effort, defining success, and turning a conceptual redesign into a production-grade framework that teams could trust and scale.

I drove it end-to-end from concept validation to delivery:
- Conducted user research, including surveys and interviews with internal and external users to capture pain points and priorities.
- Developed and tested multiple design iterations grounded in Pipedrive’s design system.
- Collaborated deeply with a multi-disciplinary team of five developers and a PM to build, maintain, and evolve the component library.
- Facilitated cross-tribe design reviews to align on shared behaviors and UI across product areas.
- Documented extensively, producing detailed guides and Confluence resources to support adoption and continuity.

## 3. SaaS Design System

### Challenge
When I joined Pipedrive, a SaaS app covering the full cycles of sales, the design component library was a legacy. It existed in Sketch in the age of Figma, consisted of no more than 15 components that were poorly structured, and the documentation was stored on a custom website that was only accessible with VPN, driving away the system’s users.

There were three main challenges:
- To create a comprehensive system starting from one library and bring the setup to a new level of maturity. 
- Improve user journeys of the users of the design system.
- Refine the team’s processes for better alignment and predictable outcomes.

### My Role
I drove the transformation of the company’s legacy design library into a scalable, mature, token-driven system. Operating in a team of two, I managed the end-to-end ecosystem, focusing on:
- System architecture and long-term roadmap.
- Cross-functional alignment with product and engineering leadership.
- Governance, contribution model, and adoption strategy.
- Project management and delivery operations.

I also planted the idea of treating the system as an internal product with its customers and supportive processes.

## 4. SaaS UX Architecture

### Challenge
Since 2019, Pipedrive has shifted its strategic focus from being a simple CRM to covering the entire sales cycle. This evolution introduced new features and apps, but the existing navigation and information architecture were not fit for the challenge, creating usability blockers for users and internal teams alike. This project aimed to rethink and redesign the navigation structure to support Pipedrive’s expanded product scope and user needs.

### My Role
As the project initiator, I took full ownership of the design process on behalf of the Design Systems team, leading end-to-end from defining the problem space to delivering interactive prototypes.

The project was a cross-functional effort, collaborating closely with two Product Managers, two UX Researchers, and one Business Analyst to align user, business, and technical perspectives.

## 5. Regional Data Marketplace

### Challenge
This system had no analogs to examine, and there were no relevant users. It accumulated data from 80+ institutions that must be checked, structured, linked, presented, and eventually selected by the user.

### My Role
The working team consisted of a PM, two developers, a test automation engineer, a business analyst, and myself.

Using the design system I created for the company, I designed a complex environment where operators create data models at the request of data owners and end users pick the data they want. The design was based on the information provided by the business analyst.

## 6. Design System for E-gov

### Challenge
As the sole designer in a company delivering digital platforms across multiple government areas, I faced a critical challenge: multiple independent departments operated without dedicated designers, resulting in a delivery of inconsistent interfaces and patterns. Many platforms were used by overlapping audiences who were forced to relearn workflows and UI each time. The need for a unified, coherent design system was urgent to streamline user experience across the organization.

### My Role
As the only designer in the org, I owned the full lifecycle of building the design system from scratch: establishing foundational design units, principles, and documentation while evangelizing the approach across decentralized teams with no prior design resources.
